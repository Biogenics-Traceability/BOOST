**Status:** DRAFT v2 — not sent. Terminology updated 2026-10-14 (#311); the body is as of June 2026 and needs a content refresh before sending.

Dear BOOST Community Group members,

A short update, and a request.

Since the v3.4.2 release last August, BOOST has moved from specification work into use. An interagency effort funded through the California Board of Forestry's Joint Institute for Wood Products Innovation and resource conservation district partners is now underway, bringing state agencies — including the Air Resources Board, the Public Utilities Commission, CAL FIRE, and the Department of Conservation — around shared data requirements for biomass chain of custody. A validation tool prototyped against the BOOST schema is moving into real-world pathway testing for the Low Carbon Fuel Standard and BioRAM programs. And WWF-US is benchmarking BOOST against its Global Traceability Framework for Timber, mapping our entities to that framework's critical tracking events and key data elements.

What the specification needs now is specific expertise, not just numbers. The next two quarters of work have named gaps, and I'd rather fill them with the right people than grow the list. We are looking for:

- people who run mill intake, scaling, or inventory systems day to day
- facility operators and pathway holders who file LCFS or BioRAM reports and could test the validation tool against a real submission
- certification-scheme technical staff (chain-of-custody practice, EUDR due diligence)
- developers with EPCIS or UN Transparency Protocol implementation experience, or who build EUDR compliance tooling
- forest biometricians and inventory specialists

**The request:** if someone in your network fits one of these, reply to me directly or make the introduction yourself — a personal introduction from a colleague converts far better than any announcement we could write. If you do make an introduction, here is a paragraph you can adapt:

> The BOOST Community Group at W3C is developing an open data specification for tracking forest biomass and wood products through the supply chain — one shared vocabulary that works across certification systems and regulatory programs, instead of a separate format for each. The specification is being tested with California state agencies and benchmarked against WWF's Global Traceability Framework for Timber. The group is looking for people who work with this data in practice: supply chain operators, certifiers, agency staff, and software developers. Membership is free and joining takes a few minutes: https://www.w3.org/community/boost-01/

Joining is free and open to anyone; substantive contributors sign the W3C Community Contributor License Agreement at sign-up. New participants can explore the data model through the interactive ERD Navigator (https://Biogenics-Traceability.github.io/BOOST/) and pick up a task or simply follow along on this list.

Thank you — the specification is only as good as the range of people who shape it.

Best regards,

Peter Tittmann
Chair, BOOST Community Group
peter@arbos.bio
